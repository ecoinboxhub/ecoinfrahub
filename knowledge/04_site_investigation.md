# Site Investigation and Soil Testing

## Investigation Phases
- Desk study: Review existing maps, aerial photos, geologic reports, site history
- Reconnaissance: Site walkover, visual assessment, access planning, utility location
- Preliminary investigation: Trial pits, hand augers, geophysical surveys
- Detailed investigation: Boreholes, sampling, in-situ testing, laboratory analysis
- Construction verification: Proof rolling, compaction testing, foundation inspection

## Borehole Requirements
- Spacing: 50-100 m for linear projects (roads, pipelines), 3-6 per structure foundation
- Depth: 1.5-2.0 times foundation width, minimum 5 m below proposed foundation level
- Minimum 3 m into competent rock or bearing stratum for end-bearing piles
- Water table: Record all groundwater strikes and 24-hour rest levels
- Core recovery: Minimum 85% for rock coring, RQD calculated for each run

## Soil Sampling
- Disturbed samples: Bulk bags for classification tests, moisture content
- Undisturbed samples: Thin-walled Shelby tubes (75 mm diameter) for soft clays
- Piston samplers: For very soft clays, peat, organic soils
- Split spoon samples: SPT test provides both sample and penetration resistance
- Sampling intervals: Every 1.5 m in homogeneous soils, at every change in stratum

## In-Situ Testing
- Standard Penetration Test (SPT): N-value for sand density and clay consistency
- Cone Penetration Test (CPT): Continuous profile of tip resistance and sleeve friction
- Vane shear test: Undrained shear strength of cohesive soils in borehole
- Plate load test: Bearing capacity and settlement modulus at foundation level
- Permeability tests: Variable head in boreholes, constant head in pervious strata
- Pressuremeter test: In-situ stress-strain modulus and lateral earth pressure

## Laboratory Soil Tests
- Particle size distribution: Sieve analysis for coarse soils, hydrometer for fine soils
- Atterberg limits: Liquid limit, plastic limit, plasticity index for clay classification
- Natural moisture content: Oven drying method, critical for compaction control
- Compaction tests: Standard Proctor and Modified Proctor for optimum moisture content
- California Bearing Ratio (CBR): Soaked and unsoaked for pavement design
- Triaxial compression: UU, CU, CD tests for shear strength parameters c and phi
- Consolidation test: Oedometer test for settlement magnitude and rate in clays

## Geotechnical Design Parameters
- Bearing capacity: Terzaghi, Meyerhof, Hansen methods for shallow foundations
- Skin friction and end bearing: Calculated from SPT N-values for pile design
- Settlement: Immediate (elastic) and consolidation settlement calculations
- Lateral earth pressure: Active, passive, at-rest coefficients for retaining structures
- Slope stability: Bishop simplified method, Fellenius method for circular slips
- Liquefaction potential: Based on SPT N-value, grain size, seismic zone

## Reporting
- Borehole logs: Standard format showing strata, SPT N-values, water levels, samples
- Geotechnical sections: Longitudinal and transverse profiles along alignment
- Design parameters table: Recommended values for bearing capacity, friction angle, cohesion
- Foundation recommendations: Type, depth, allowable bearing pressure, settlement estimate
- Construction considerations: Dewatering needs, excavation support, groundwater control
- Laboratory test results: All test data summarized in appendices with standards referenced

## Quality Control During Construction
- Proof rolling: Heavy roller passes to identify soft spots in subgrade
- Compaction testing: Field density by sand replacement or nuclear gauge
- Plate load tests: Verify bearing capacity at foundation formation level
- Pile integrity testing: Sonic echo or cross-hole sonic logging for cast-in-situ piles
- Pile load tests: Static or dynamic load tests for pile capacity verification
- Settlement monitoring: Survey markers during and after construction
